Why programs call us

Fragmented pilots don’t survive. Systems do.

Most MIH programs don’t fail on care — they fail on design. The visit works, but the funding is one grant deep, the data doesn’t prove anything, and the workflow lives in one person’s head. We fix the system underneath the care.

Find the frequent callers, the avoidable transports, and the gaps in primary and behavioral health access driving volume — then design visits that actually change that pattern.

OUTCOME 02

Improve access

Bring care to people where they are: home visits, mobile clinic days, and follow-up that closes the loop for patients who otherwise fall through — especially in rural and underserved areas.

OUTCOME 03

Survive beyond the grant

Build a payer and funding mix, documented avoided cost, and stakeholder reporting that make the case for keeping the program — without relying on a single grant to carry it.

How we think

Three ideas we bring to every engagement

“Billing alone doesn’t prove value. Documented avoided cost does.”

A transport avoided, an ED visit prevented, a readmission that never happened — if it isn’t documented, it didn’t happen for the funder. We build that documentation into the workflow from the start.

“Sort patients by slope, not just volume.”

The highest-volume caller isn’t always the highest-value enrollment. Trajectory matters — who is getting worse, who is stabilizing, and where a visit today changes next month’s calls.

“Build the proof in month one.”

Programs don’t get to retrofit evidence at grant-report time. Dashboards, definitions, and reporting start with the first visit, so renewal is a summary — not a scramble.
